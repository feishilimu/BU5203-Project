## A. Regulatory developments

### Singapore

**What is covered**

- **[sg-srf-guidelines-1]** Holders of credit cards, charge cards, and debit cards in Singapore benefit from liability apportionment rules under the ABS Code of Practice for Banks – Credit Cards and existing fraud prevention measures. (status: in force)
  - Source: [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (MAS & IMDA)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework) p.2
  - Quote: “1 Holders of credit cards, charge cards and debit cards issued in Singapore currently benefit from liability apportionment in the ABS Code of Practice for Banks – Credit Cards, and existing fraud prevention measures in place.”
  - **Reviewer: follow-up** – Incomplete: the key point is that the SRF excludes card transactions, see footnote 1. · ⚠️ no_date_for_rule (low)

**Duties on banks**

- **[sg-srf-guidelines-3]** In a phishing scam scenario where a protected account is rapidly drained, the financial institution must either block the transaction and subsequent payments to the scammer until verified, or notify the customer and block/hold the transaction for at least 24 hours. (status: in force)
  - Source: [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (MAS & IMDA)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework) p.8
  - Quote: “(a) Block the online banking payment transaction that would result in the crossing of the threshold set out in footnote 8, and all subsequent online banking payment transactions to the scammer until it is able to obtain further verification from the account holder; or (b) Send notification to the account holder, and block or hold for at least 24 hours:”
  - **Reviewer: accepted** · ⚠️ no_date_for_rule (low)

**Who bears the loss**

- **[sg-srf-guidelines-5]** Financial institutions must bear losses from seemingly authorised transactions if the loss is caused by their own non-compliance with specified duties. (status: in force)
  - Source: [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (MAS & IMDA)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework) p.10
  - Quote: “6.2 The responsible FI is expected to bear any loss arising from a seemingly authorised transaction if the loss arises from any non-compliance by the responsible FI with any duty set out in paragraph 4.2 of these Guidelines.”
  - **Reviewer: follow-up** · ⚠️ no_date_for_rule (low)
- **[sg-srf-guidelines-6]** Telcos must bear losses from SMS-perpetrated seemingly authorised transactions if the financial institution complied with its duties and the loss resulted from the telco's non-compliance. (status: in force)
  - Source: [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (MAS & IMDA)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework) p.10
  - Quote: “6.4 The responsible Telco is expected to bear losses arising from a seemingly authorised transaction perpetrated through SMS if: (a) the responsible FI has complied with all of its duties set out in paragraph 4.2 of these Guidelines; and (b) the loss arises from the responsible Telco’s non-compliance with any duty set out in paragraph 5.2 of these Guidelines.”
  - **Reviewer: follow-up** · ⚠️ no_date_for_rule (low)
- **[sg-srf-guidelines-7]** Account holders of protected accounts must bear losses from seemingly authorised transactions if neither the financial institution nor the telco is found liable under their respective duties. (status: in force)
  - Source: [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (MAS & IMDA)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework) p.11
  - Quote: “6.7 Where the responsible FI is not expected to bear the loss under paragraph 6.2 of these Guidelines, and the responsible Telco is not expected to bear the loss under paragraph 6.4 of these Guidelines, the account holder of a protected account should bear any loss arising from a seemingly authorised transaction.”
  - **Reviewer: accepted** · ⚠️ no_date_for_rule (low)

**Claims process and timelines**

- **[sg-srf-guidelines-8]** The financial institution serves as the first and overall point of contact for account holders processing claims for seemingly authorised transactions. (status: in force)
  - Source: [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (MAS & IMDA)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework) p.11
  - Quote: “(a) Claim Stage 7.1.1 A responsible FI is the first and overall point of contact with the account holder.”
  - **Reviewer: accepted** · ⚠️ no_date_for_rule (low)
- **[sg-srf-guidelines-9]** When a scam is perpetrated through SMS, both the financial institution and the telco conduct concurrent investigations into whether they fulfilled their respective duties. (status: in force)
  - Source: [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (MAS & IMDA)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework) p.13
  - Quote: “7.7.1 The responsible FI and responsible Telco investigate concurrently whether each of them has fulfilled their duties under paragraphs 4.2 and 5.2, respectively.”
  - **Reviewer: accepted** · ⚠️ no_date_for_rule (low)
